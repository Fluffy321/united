# Five Towns Directory Source Audit

Checked: August 28, 2026

## Coverage

The production dataset contains 138 sourced records: the original Five Towns prototype, researched records that fill the previously missing health, professional-services, family, shopping, and activity groups, and three official local recreation listings. Thirty-seven high-confidence records are marked as featured and include source-linked, fact-based `Why go` guidance.

The user-facing directory groups are:

- Jewish life
- Food
- Family
- Shopping
- Health
- Services
- Community
- Things to do

The dataset is intentionally not described as a complete list of every Five Towns business. JUnited favors records with a public source and an actionable location over unsourced volume.

## Source order

Facts are checked in this order:

1. Current kosher-certifier listing for a kosher claim.
2. Official business, practice, school, camp, shul, organization, or government website.
3. Official local business district or community directory for discovery and basic public contact information.
4. Aggregator sources only when a stronger public source is not available; aggregator-only records do not receive a verified kosher badge.

## Kosher verification

JUnited shows a verified kosher label only when the record contains a current public certification source. The initial verified records use listing pages from the Five Towns & Far Rockaway Vaad Hakashrus at `https://vaadhakashrus.org/`.

Food listings without a current certifier source remain searchable but do not receive a verified kosher badge. A business description or an older third-party directory claim is not enough to display certification.

## Important meaning of inclusion

A non-food listing is included because it publicly operates in or serves the Five Towns and fits a useful directory category. Inclusion does not claim that its owners or staff are Jewish. JUnited does not infer religion or ownership from a name, neighborhood, or customer base.

## Correction policy

Users can report a closed, moved, renamed, miscategorized, or certification-changed listing. A correction should preserve the old source for audit purposes, add the new source, and update the `lastChecked` date. A disputed kosher claim should be hidden until a current certifier source is confirmed.
